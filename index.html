Getting there 出発

We meet up, and the tides decide the rest. At high tide there's a jet boat — the fastest way to the island, 45 minutes, ¥2,500 per person one way. Otherwise we take the train (55 minutes, ¥250 per person) and then the ferry (15 minutes, ¥350 per person round trip). Fares are not included in the tour price.

The island tour

Itsukushima & Miyajima: The Shrine Island

I've had guests tell me, upon walking around on Itsukushima: "NOW I feel like I'm in Japan!" You can feel the history here. The sights and smells are everywhere — the oysters are being grilled, the stuffed maple cakes are being baked (and then deep fried!), the fish cakes are being broiled, the eel is being grilled, and don't even get me started on all the lemonade (both with alcohol and without). And it's ALL FOR YOU!

That's just the walk up to the UNESCO World Heritage site — one of the top three most photographed places in all of Japan. Well, you do have some competition: the island's indigenous deer population might try to pilfer your tasty treats. But that's why you have a professional tour guide to save you! No matter the season, Itsukushima is vibrant with color and teeming with life.

The city tour

Peace Park & Surrounds

I. LOVE. HIROSHIMA!! I fell in love with this city when I came here for work in the summer of 2001 — living about 70 km away, visiting maybe twice a month, speaking almost no Japanese and reading even less. I met my wife here. I've raised two children here. I consider this my home now, and I love to show people around my home.

I love explaining the history and the culture — not just the reason everyone in the world knows the name of our city, but our lemons, our shellfish, our industry, our baseball and soccer teams. We will definitely discuss some sad and disturbing topics on this tour, but we will also discover the true spirit of Hiroshima and the Japanese people. You will come out of this with a greater understanding of why Hiroshima is known as a city of peace — and why we say: No more Hiroshimas!
